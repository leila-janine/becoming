## A practical bridge toward AI engineering

1. Use current paid work to deepen end-to-end delivery: requirements, implementation, validation, review, and handoff. Only take on AI or data changes within an approved work scope. Keep employer materials confidential.
2. Improve one existing AI project rather than starting several unrelated demos. Your local-manual RAG is a candidate: add reproducible ingestion, retrieval and grounding evaluation, failure cases, a tested API, and deployment notes.
3. Add review or real use when feasible. A scoped open-source contribution or stakeholder-approved pilot can provide feedback and accountability. Describe it accurately; do not relabel it as employment. Unpaid work is optional, not a prerequisite.
4. Search graduate, associate, junior AI/ML, AI application, and MLOps roles, along with relevant Python/data roles. Treat these as search terms, not verified vacancies. Keep AI as the main destination and use supporting roles deliberately.
5. During graduation and moving weeks, aim for a small technical improvement, an evidence note, and one or two thoughtful job-description reviews when capacity allows. Avoid turning four career interests into four daily study obligations.

## Resume evidence to check before applications

Keep exact credential names, award wording, dates, contribution boundaries, and measured outcomes accurate. Claims such as “zero crashes,” “100% accuracy,” or percentage improvements should identify their measurement period, denominator, and evidence. Research model metrics should name the dataset split and evaluation conditions; they should not imply clinical validation or deployment outcomes that were not established.
